The roster

Seven ways to own a megawatt

1870 – 1911

Standard Oil

  • ~90% of US refining at peak (common historical record)
  • Broken up 1911 into 34 companies
  • Descendants include ExxonMobil and Chevron

Unit-cost obsession plus vertical integration as the original energy moat.

1892 – 1932

Insull / Commonwealth Edison

  • Invented the modern utility business model
  • Load diversity and load-factor pricing electrified Chicago
  • Holding-company pyramid collapsed in 1932

Aggregate diverse loads and the same megawatt earns many times. Home-battery fleets re-run this play.

1933 – now

Saudi Aramco

  • $1.71T market cap (companiesmarketcap.com, 2026-08-14)
  • The lowest-cost barrels on earth, at scale

Resource-cost advantage survives every cycle.

1911 – now

ExxonMobil

  • $652B market cap (companiesmarketcap.com, 2026-08-14)
  • Direct Standard Oil descendant, a century of shocks survived

Capital discipline compounds longer than any single energy era lasts.

2003 – now

Tesla (Energy)

  • $1.34T market cap (companiesmarketcap.com, 2026-08-14)
  • Megapack and Powerwall: storage as a product line

Manufacturing scale applied to batteries turns energy into a factory problem.

2023 – now

Base Power

  • $13B valuation, Series D Aug 2026 (Forbes, Sourcery)
  • 500+ MWh deployed, 30,000+ homes (Electrek 2026-08-03, careers page)
  • Home batteries plus a retail electricity plan, ERCOT

Insull's load diversity, rebuilt with batteries and software.

2015 – now

Octopus Energy

  • Kraken platform licensed to competing utilities
  • Study in progress, sourced numbers landing soon

The software layer can outgrow the energy company that built it.
