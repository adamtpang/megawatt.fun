How the rules work

Program size

Batteries = MW x 1,000 / 20, rounded up. Storage MWh = batteries x 39.2 / 1,000. Share of homes = batteries / residential customers. The 20 kW and 39.2 kWh figures are Base's published spec for its home battery.

Percentiles

Each signal is compared with every utility in the file that publishes it. "Higher than 80% of US utilities" means 80% of those utilities report a lower value. Missing values are shown as "not published" and are never treated as zero.

Headline

The headline uses the utility's strongest public signal: whichever of outage minutes (SAIDI), outage count (SAIFI), storm risk, heat-wave risk, or demand-response enrollment has the highest national percentile. Ties go in that order.

Offer

  1. If you mark that large loads are waiting to connect: Speed to power. No public file says this, so it is your input.
  2. Else, if outage minutes or outage count is higher than 75% of US utilities: Distribution grid support. The 75% cutoff is an assumption.
  3. Else, if at least half of residential customers sit in an ISO or RTO balancing authority: Bulk peaking capacity. In ERCOT, which runs an energy-only market, the fit is peak shaving. Elsewhere it is accredited capacity.
  4. Else: Bulk peaking capacity, as peak shaving to reduce the capacity the utility must plan for.

Who to call first

Score = residential customers x SAIDI minutes. That is a residential stand-in for customer minutes interrupted, the total outage time a utility's homes sit through in a year. It favors big utilities with long outages. SAIDI covers all customers, so the product is an approximation. Utilities with no published SAIDI go last.
