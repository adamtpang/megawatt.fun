Make, move, store, sell

Zach Dell’s stack has four jobs. The Kardashev number only counts one of them directly: primary energy a civilization uses. This is an outside ranking of those jobs, and of the public products that sit on them.

The question

On this site, K = (log10(P) − 6) / 10. P is average primary power. The 2024 world row used here is 176,737 TWh over a leap year, 20.12 TW, K 0.730. Type I is 10,000 TW. A tenth on the scale is ten times the power.

A battery does not add primary energy. It holds energy some generator already made, and gives it back later. That raises K only when the battery causes energy to be generated or used that otherwise would not have been: spilled noon solar that finds an evening load, or a new generator that gets built because something firms its shape. Backup hours are real for the house. They are energy that was already counted when it was made.

How much power moves the number

New average primary power, at the 2024 baseline. This is generation that gets used. It is not battery nameplate.

+0.001 K needs 469 GW of average new primary power.

About 206 MW is the nameplate on Base’s own August 2026 fleet chart, a partial month, with partner-utility fleets left out. That is storage, and it is more than two thousand times smaller than 469 GW. The chart is nameplate, not telemetered capability.

The four jobs

Packy McCormick quotes Dell: “You think about the stack and power, it’s really four things: you have to make it (generation), you have to move it (transmission and distribution), you have to store it (batteries) and you have to sell it (retail and wholesale markets).” A Dealroom summary of Dell’s Founders interview says the company entered through store and sell, where scale and return looked best, and that the ambition runs across all four. Packy also writes that Dell has hinted at owning generation. This page does not treat that ambition as a published build schedule.

What to optimize

S changes the primary power that gets used. A makes S possible or pays for it. B grows the company on energy that was coming anyway. Later is a means, not a job on the scale.

SThe numerator

MakeGeneration

New primary energy that is actually used is the only job that adds joules to P. Public accounts say Base entered elsewhere and that Dell has talked about owning generation. There is no plant list here. The version that climbs is a book that is short power, covered by generation someone builds because the contract exists. The battery’s job in that version is to firm the shape so the generator can be financed.

MoveWires, and the wait to connect

Energy that cannot reach a load does not count. This site’s other bottleneck is plugging in, not only building. Base is not, on the sources below, a transmission company. The public substitute is the battery at the house: a Dealroom summary of the same interview says distributed batteries sidestep interconnection limits and congestion by sitting at the load and shifting midday solar toward the evening peak. That is store doing part of move’s work. It climbs when it delivers energy a constraint was blocking. It is not a new line. The ERCOT note is the public map of where day-ahead constraints were asking for that. Phase 4 would still pay the zone price, so the spread is not a payoff.

AAlready shipping

StoreThe battery they own

This is the product. Base installs a battery, keeps it, and operates it. The Dealroom summary says that asset works in the wholesale market 99% of the time the grid is up, and backs up the house when the grid is down. Storage lets variable generation be used. It does not add primary energy. Point it at hours that would otherwise be spilled, and at places a constraint is binding. Backup is why a home will take the box. It is the small slice of the battery’s year.

SellThe electricity, not the box

In most of Texas the public model is a retailer that also runs the fleet in the wholesale market. Where the utility is regulated, the public account is a sale to municipal utilities and co-ops, on the order of a decade of megawatts under contract. Selling adds no joules. It is how make and store get paid. Packy describes the retailer as short power, because it has promised to serve homes, and the fleet as long power, because it holds energy it can dispatch. That pairing is the commercial design that can cause generation. A plan that only sells outage coverage is a B.

BReal, small on K

More backupHomes, boxes, states

More installations matter to the company and to the houses that lose power. If the energy in the battery was going to be generated and consumed anyway, the Kardashev numerator does not move. The state list is about where a battery program has a public case. It is not a ranking of where new primary energy would change K the most.

LaterThe means

Factory, software, costVertical integration

Dell describes a cost advantage from designing, building, installing, and selling under one roof. That is how store, and later make, can get cheap enough to exist in volume. It is not a fifth job on the scale. A cheaper battery climbs K only by showing up in make, move, store, or sell.

The order, if the goal is K

Make energy that gets delivered. Move energy that is stuck. Use store and sell to cause those two. The battery and the retail plan are the A that is already public. They become S when the book causes new generation, and when the fleet is placed and dispatched where energy cannot get through. Factory throughput is how many of those batteries exist. It is not the climb by itself.

Nothing here is a megawatt target for Base, a forecast of their roadmap, or a claim that a home fleet moves humanity a measurable step toward Type I. At 20 TW, even the S jobs have to be enormous before K notices. The ranking says which jobs are worth aiming at. It does not say a company of this size closes the gap.

What this is not

Independent analysis, not affiliated with Base Power, a utility, or a market operator. No internal plan was used. Primary energy is not electricity, and a battery’s nameplate is not average generation. The 99% figure, the regulated-market contracts, and the “entered through store and sell” line are from a Dealroom summary of a podcast, not from a filing. The four-job sentence is Dell’s, as published by Not Boring on 12 August 2026. The 469 GW figure is this site’s formula at the 2024 baseline. The August 2026 fleet chart is Base’s, and it says the series is nameplate.
