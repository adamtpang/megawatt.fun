What this suggests

At equal weights, Michigan (76), Texas (71), and Maryland (69) lead the 51 geographies. Michigan's strongest public signals on this scale are outage duration (saidi) (98) and demand response per 1,000 customers (92). Read the score as a stack of percentiles. A high score means the public series line up. It is an outside view, with no Base install times, permit outcomes, or crew plan in it.

The home-count factor rewards size. California has the most owner-occupied single-family homes, 6,771,188. With that weight set to zero, the top three become Michigan (75), Maryland (70), and Texas (68).

Base's public footprint, marked on the map and kept out of the weights: Texas ranks 2 (score 71), with SAIDI 225 minutes, retail delivery share 56.2%, and wholesale mix ERCOT 88%, MISO 5%, SPP 5%. Illinois ranks 8 (score 60), with SAIDI 144 minutes, retail delivery share 22.1%, and wholesale mix PJM 71%, MISO 29%. Georgia ranks 30 (score 47), with SAIDI 270 minutes, retail delivery share 0.0%, and wholesale mix No ISO balancing authority. Connecticut ranks 19 (score 54), with SAIDI 211 minutes, retail delivery share 15.8%, and wholesale mix ISO-NE 100%.

Georgia's largest balancing authority in this file is Southern Company Services, Inc. - Trans. EIA reports no residential delivery service there. A utility contract, the shape of the reported Central Georgia EMC project, does not appear in the retail-choice factor, so Georgia's rank is a weak guide to that path.

The longest state SAIDI in this file is West Virginia at 1013 minutes. The highest residential delivery share is Massachusetts at 61.5%. Commonwealth Edison reports 3,753,924 residential customers, SAIDI 77 minutes, and wholesale tag PJM. Central Georgia (Central Georgia El Member Corp) reports 62,546 residential customers and SAIDI 168 minutes.
