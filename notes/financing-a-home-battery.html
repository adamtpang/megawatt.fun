Financing a home battery

What a lender could see from public prices: one battery, settled at the ERCOT load zone, across 2018 through 2025, including the extreme years. Base’s debt structure and fleet dispatch are not public. This is an outside view.

Loading the price years…

The question

A home battery that is paid the load-zone price earns the gap between the hours it charges and the hours it discharges. Report 13060 is the public day-ahead history of those load-zone prices back to 2011. This note uses the eight complete years 2018 through 2025. 2021 and 2023 are in the set. 2026 is a partial file and is left out.

The battery in the default run is a Base Core’s published 39.2 kWh. Continuous kilowatts, round-trip efficiency, fade, and cost are not on that spec sheet, so those inputs start as labeled illustrations. Changing them recomputes the debt math on the revenues below. It does not rerun the dispatch. The dispatch was run once, at the defaults, by analysis/build_financing.py.

How the two problems connect

A new market that takes longer to open does not change the price history. It changes when the first operating year begins. In this illustration the loan balance grows at the stated interest rate during that wait, and the payment is sized off the larger balance. The same median revenue then covers less of the payment. The state list is the public evidence on which launches have a retail-choice path and which ones wait on a utility partnership. It is not a measured count of months.

Revenue here is also not a forecast of what the battery will earn next year. It is what this dispatch would have earned in each historical year. 2021 is in the distribution on purpose. The median is the number to read. The mean is pulled by the extreme year.

What I couldn't see

  • Base’s actual debt structure, interest rate, tenor, advance rate, and covenants.
  • What the battery cost Base to build. The $695 installation fee and $19 a month on the public site are customer prices, not capital cost. NREL ATB’s residential-battery workbook was not read into a single cost row for this build, so the $/kWh slider starts at zero and debt service stays blank until you move it.
  • Continuous kilowatts, round-trip efficiency, cycle life, and fade. The Core spec sheet publishes 39.2 kWh, 120/240 V, and backup duration. The 9.8 kW default is 39.2 kWh divided by 4 hours.
  • Fleet dispatch, reserve policy, and outage constraints. This run holds nothing back for backup and sees every future price in the year.
  • Real-time prices, nodal prices, and ancillary-service revenue. Report 13060 is day-ahead hubs and load zones.
  • A 10-year Treasury yield, if the public CSV did not load. The page says so in the loan note.
