Battery debt financing

How much a lender could lend against one leased home battery, and against a fleet, from the cash the battery earns, after the member's payment and the federal storage tax credit. Change any input. The page redoes the math as you type.

A generic model, not any company's books. This site is not affiliated with Base Power or any battery company. Public prices and specs are used as example inputs only. Lender terms, the toll and operating cost are placeholders.

What drives the default answer: member fees and ERCOT energy arbitrage (both public numbers) carry the loan, and the 30% tax credit cuts what must be funded. The default case is merchant, so the utility toll placeholder does not count. The first line under "What this means" names the biggest input for whatever you set.

Each input says Sourced (public number with a link) or Assumption (our placeholder).

      Method and what is left out

      • Debt sizing. Level yearly payment over the loan term. The payment is the weakest year's CFADS inside the term, divided by the target DSCR. Debt is the present value of that payment at the interest rate. Then the loan is capped at the advance rate times installed cost, and never above cost minus the member's upfront payment.
      • Tax credit. The federal investment tax credit for energy storage (IRC 48E) goes to the owner of a leased battery. The model treats it the simplest standard way: the owner sells the credit for cash at face value when the battery goes into service (a transfer under IRC 6418), and that cash lowers the net cost that debt and equity must fund. Left out: the discount buyers pay for credits (often several cents per dollar; lower the percent to test it), the months between paying for the battery and getting paid for the credit (usually a bridge loan), five-year recapture risk, the depreciation basis cut, bonus adders, and tax equity partnerships.
      • Equity per battery = installed cost minus member upfront payment minus tax credit minus debt.
      • Fade cuts toll and arbitrage each year. Member fees stay flat. No inflation on anything.
      • Energy arbitrage uses a fixed daily clock: charge 10 AM to 2 PM, discharge 7 to 9 PM, Central time, every day, settled at the load zone real-time price. The value comes from the zone value file, which shows the schedule and method. No ancillary services, no capacity value, no retail revenue. It is not any company's revenue.
      • Inputs are checked the same way on this page and in the API: plain decimal numbers within each field's limits. A blank field keeps its default. An invalid field shows an error and the results wait until it is fixed.
      • Not modeled: income taxes and depreciation, tax equity, member churn and defaults, reserve accounts, fees on the loan, sculpted payments, refinancing, and the value of the battery after the loan ends. Each of these can move the answer a lot.
      • Fleet is the unit result times a count. Real lenders look at the whole pool, with concentration limits and performance history.
