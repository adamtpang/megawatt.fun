Battery debt financing

How much a lender could lend against one leased home battery, and against a fleet, from the cash the battery earns, after the member's payment and the federal storage tax credit. Change any input. The page redoes the math as you type.

A generic model, not any company's books. This site is not affiliated with Base Power or any battery company. Public prices and specs are used as example inputs only. Lender terms, the toll and operating cost are placeholders.

What drives the default answer: member fees and ERCOT energy arbitrage (both public numbers) carry the loan, and the 30% tax credit, sold at a market discount, cuts what must be funded. The default case is merchant, so the utility toll placeholder does not count. The first line under "What this means" names the biggest input for whatever you set.

Each input says Sourced (public number with a link) or Assumption (our placeholder).

What earns the cash

Base says it can "toll Base-owned batteries to the utility on a pay-for-performance basis, or transfer ownership of the fleet to the utility once deployed (build-transfer model)" (Base, utilities page, retrieved 2026-10-29). Whoever holds the toll usually controls dispatch, so toll and arbitrage rarely both go to the owner.

Energy arbitrage only, 12 real ERCOT months, from our zone value file.

Export and ADER. At up to 20 kW from 7 to 9 PM, most of the discharge is more than a home uses, so it flows out to the grid. Behind the meter, that export earns the load-zone price only if the battery is part of an aggregation in ERCOT's Aggregate Distributed Energy Resource (ADER) pilot, which settles it in the wholesale market, or if the retail provider buys it back at that price. The same pilot is the route to ancillary services (Non-Spin and ECRS so far) for home batteries. Without either, the arbitrage line overstates what the battery earns.

      Method and what is left out

      • Debt sizing. Level yearly payment over the loan term. The payment is the weakest year's CFADS inside the term, divided by the target DSCR. Debt is the present value of that payment at the interest rate. Then the loan is capped at the advance rate times installed cost, and never above cost minus the member's upfront payment.
      • Tax credit. The federal investment tax credit for energy storage (IRC 48E) goes to the owner of a leased battery. The model treats it the simplest standard way: the owner sells the credit for cash when the battery goes into service (a transfer under IRC 6418), at the credit sale price (default 89.5 cents per dollar, Crux's 2026-vintage ITC average), and that cash lowers the net cost that debt and equity must fund. Left out: the months between paying for the battery and getting paid for the credit (usually a bridge loan), five-year recapture risk and the insurance buyers ask for, the depreciation basis cut, bonus adders, and tax equity partnerships.
      • Equity per battery = installed cost minus member upfront payment minus tax credit minus debt.
      • Fade cuts toll and arbitrage each year. Member fees stay flat. No inflation on anything.
      • Energy arbitrage uses a fixed daily clock: charge 10 AM to 2 PM, discharge 7 to 9 PM, Central time, every day, settled at the load zone real-time price. The value comes from the zone value file, which shows the schedule and method. Most of that discharge is export, which earns the load-zone price only through an ERCOT ADER aggregation or a provider buyback. No capacity value, no retail revenue. It is not any company's revenue.
      • Ancillary services (ERCOT reserves such as Non-Spin and ECRS, reached by home batteries only through the ADER pilot) are one input in $ per kW-year, $0 by default because no public number gives what an aggregated home battery nets. A battery cannot sell the same kWh twice: a kW held as reserve in an hour cannot also discharge for arbitrage that hour. So the input is the extra value after the arbitrage it displaces (for example, reserve sold in the 22 hours outside the discharge window), and the model adds it to arbitrage. It counts in the merchant and stacked cases, not the toll case, with availability and fade applied.
      • Inputs are checked the same way on this page and in the API: plain decimal numbers within each field's limits. A blank field keeps its default. An invalid field shows an error and the results wait until it is fixed.
      • Depreciation is not modeled. It is a tax deduction, not cash the battery earns, so it does not change CFADS or the debt sized on CFADS; it changes the owner's taxes and so the equity return.
      • Not modeled: income taxes, tax equity, member churn and defaults, reserve accounts, fees on the loan, sculpted payments, refinancing, and the value of the battery after the loan ends. Each of these can move the answer a lot.
      • Fleet is the unit result times a count. Real lenders look at the whole pool, with concentration limits and performance history.
