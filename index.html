1 · What is the Kardashev scale?

A civilization's level is how much power it can use.

The Kardashev scale ranks a civilization by the power it harnesses: a planet's worth, a star's worth, or a galaxy's worth.

Power means energy per second, measured in watts (W). A kettle uses about 2,000 W.

Type III1036 W · a galaxy
Type II1026 W · a star
Type I1016 W · a planet
Type 0106 W · a small town
K = 0.73 · about 20 TW

K = (log10(P) − 6) / 10, with P in watts1

Each step of 0.1 on K means 10 times more power. So the scale climbs like a staircase of powers of ten, not a straight line.

3 · Why does it matter?

Richer countries use more energy. Almost without exception.

Across 191 countries, energy use per person tracks income per person closely. On a log scale the correlation is 0.93.3

Correlation is not a law of physics. Some countries do more with less energy, and rich countries can cut waste. But no country has become rich while staying energy-poor.

6 · How a battery network works

Thousands of home batteries can act like one power plant.

Each home gets backup. Together, they charge when power is cheap and send power to the grid when it's short.

…

A real example: Base Power, in Texas, sells the Base Core home battery in 39.2 kWh and 78.4 kWh sizes.10 Base says the battery "powers your home and strengthens the grid".12 This site is not affiliated with Base. The animation is a simplified picture, not Base's real fleet.

7 · Play grid operator

Charge low. Sell high. Keep a backup.

ERCOT runs the Texas grid, where the price of power changes every hour.13

These are real ERCOT prices for 13 August 2026.14 Your battery holds 25 kWh, moves 5 kWh an hour, and loses 12% on the round trip. Tap an hour: idle → charge → sell.

idlecharge (buy)sell
Tap some hours to start.

Price swings alone earn well under a dollar a day. Paying off the hardware costs about $0.68 a day.15 That's why real battery networks also get paid for backup and grid services.
