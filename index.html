What to know before trusting these numbers

    Policy risk. This is already the rule in Ohio: AEP Ohio, FirstEnergy Ohio and AES Ohio recover transmission from every customer through a nonbypassable rider, so the page counts their transmission tags as 0. PPL Electric has proposed the same in Pennsylvania: transmission (NITS) as a charge every customer pays the utility, whatever their supplier. A supplier could then no longer save its members money by cutting their transmission tags. Base asked the Pennsylvania PUC to let suppliers opt out and take on those costs themselves (dockets P-2026-3065185, P-2026-3065186 and P-2024-3047290). Tick “Model PPL’s nonbypassable transmission proposal” under “Change the other assumptions” to see what the PPL zone is worth without it.

    In comments to the Pennsylvania PUC, Base said it saves customers money by dispatching batteries to lower the capacity and transmission tags (PLC and NSPL) on its customers’ bills (Energy Choice Matters, 2 Nov 2026). This page values those two tags, plus energy, for one battery in every PJM zone.
